Buddhist Transmission: Luoyang × Kyoto

Tracing the spiritual journey from the White Horse Temple to the Zen gardens of Japan.

From Chinese Doctrine to Japanese Zen

The transmission of Buddhism from China to Japan was a centuries-long process that transformed both the religion and the cultures it touched. Starting with the founding of the White Horse Temple (Baima Temple) in Luoyang in 68 AD—the first Buddhist temple in China—Buddhism served as a vessel for the sophisticated philosophical and administrative systems of the Middle Kingdom to flow eastward.

When these teachings arrived in Japan, they evolved from elaborate continental rituals into the stark, meditative traditions of Zen Buddhism. Zen took root in the monastic environment of Kyoto, emphasizing direct experience, mindfulness, and the harmony between the mind and the natural world. This evolution wasn't just a change in faith, but a synthesis—it blended Chinese intellectual rigor with a distinctly Japanese aesthetic, permanently shaping the architecture, tea culture, and philosophical landscape of modern Japan.
